# VPN client cannot connect to a Meridian private network

Applies when the Meridian VPN client reports `TLS handshake failed` or hangs at
`Verifying peer` when connecting to a private network endpoint.

## Cause

Since the 2026.06 client release, Meridian private networks require mutual TLS with a
device certificate issued by Meridian Device Trust. Certificates expire every 90 days.
An expired or missing device certificate is the cause in the large majority of cases.

## Resolution

1. Confirm certificate validity in the client under **Settings > Device Trust**. An
   expired certificate shows a red status with the expiry date.
2. Re-enroll the device: `meridian-vpn enroll --force`. This issues a new 90-day
   certificate and does not require an administrator.
3. Reconnect. If the handshake still fails, confirm the network allows outbound
   `udp/51820` and `tcp/443` to `vpn.<region>.meridiancloud.example`.
4. If the client connects but private hostnames do not resolve, the problem is DNS
   rather than VPN. See `net-002`.

## Escalate when

- Re-enrollment fails with `DEVICE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, which indicates an MDM posture
  problem that support cannot resolve.
- More than one user on the same network segment is affected at once, which suggests
  an egress firewall change rather than a client problem.

Escalate to the Network Access team with the client log bundle from
`meridian-vpn diagnose --bundle`.
