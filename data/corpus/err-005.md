# Service returns 502 or 503 under load

Applies when a healthy service begins returning 502 or 503 as traffic rises.

## Distinguish the two

- **503** is emitted by the load balancer when no worker is accepting connections.
  Usually every worker is saturated or restarting.
- **502** means a worker accepted the connection and then closed it, typically from an
  application crash or a response exceeding the 60-second gateway timeout.

## Resolution

1. Call `get_status` and read `workers.ready` against `workers.desired`. If ready is
   below desired, the service is under-provisioned for current traffic.
2. Check `metrics.p95_latency_ms`. A p95 approaching 60000 points at the gateway
   timeout as the source of 502s.
3. Scale out. Raising worker count is the correct first action for a saturated
   service and does not interrupt existing connections.
4. Inspect worker logs for `OOMKilled`. A worker being killed and rescheduled produces
   both 502s and 503s in a repeating pattern, and needs a memory-limit increase
   rather than more workers.

## Restart guidance

A restart is appropriate only when workers are wedged: ready count is zero, the
process is not serving, and logs show no progress. A restart under load makes 503s
worse, because it removes the remaining serving capacity.

Restarts are a gated action. They require the `sre` role and explicit human
confirmation, and they are recorded in the audit log. See `docs/THREAT_MODEL.md`.

## Escalate when

`OOMKilled` recurs after a memory-limit increase, which suggests a leak and needs the
owning service team.
